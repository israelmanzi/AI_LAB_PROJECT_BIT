# 2.1 Arrays and Linked Lists

> **Status:** outline only · **Owner:** _TBD_ · **Reviewer:** _TBD_

## Learning outcomes
- Explain how arrays and linked lists store items.
- Compare their cost for access, insertion and deletion.

## Key ideas
- Array: items sit next to each other in memory. Access by index is O(1), but inserting in the middle is O(n).
- Linked list: each node points to the next one. Inserting is cheap once you are at the right place, but reaching item k is O(n).
- Matrices are 2-D arrays. Neural-network weights are stored this way (3.3).

## Terms to define
array, index, node, pointer, linked list

## Worked example (planned)
Insert 15 into the middle of a 6-item array and of a 6-item linked list, counting the moves.
